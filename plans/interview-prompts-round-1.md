# EmotiTone Solfege Interview Prompts - Round 1

These prompts are designed to recover the thinking behind visible shifts in the repo. They are intentionally specific so the answers produce memory, not generic retrospection.

## 1. Note Pads As Act Zero

- In the original `note-pads` phase, what did "small enough to use immediately" mean to you in practice?
- When you were looking at Koala and Ableton Note, what exact feelings or interaction qualities were you chasing?
- Did you think of the original app as a sketchpad, a toy, a practice tool, or an instrument? Or did that category itself feel unresolved?
- The early repo adds microphone control surprisingly fast. What were you imagining the mic would unlock?

## 2. Solfege Becomes The Center

- June 23, 2025 is the first clear git trace of the emotional solfege language. What had clicked for you just before that?
- Was there a specific musical realization that made scale degrees feel more real than note names?
- Did the emotional words come after the theory clicked, or did language help the theory click?
- When you were mapping degree to color and "feel," were you aiming for pedagogy, synesthesia, composition help, or all three?

## 3. The Floating Popup Evolution

- On June 22, 2025 the popup starts as a single-note emotional display. By June 26 it is already detecting chords, and by June 26 to June 28 it is showing intervals and configurable emotional description. What changed in your mental model that week?
- Did the popup feel like the heart of the app for a while, or more like a supporting layer?
- When you imagine reintegrating it now, do you want it to teach, to interpret, to reward, or to aesthetically frame the act of playing?
- Were there moments when the popup felt too "explainy" or intrusive, even if the idea was right?

## 4. Visual Language And Hilbert Scope

- The Hilbert scope work feels unusually intentional, including a standalone prototype file. What did you see in that visual form that other visual experiments were not giving you?
- Were you trying to represent timbre, harmony, motion through space, or something more intuitive than any of those terms?
- Which visual experiments felt closest to the app's truth, and which ones were beautiful but somehow wrong?
- When visuals failed, what was usually off: legibility, feel, performance, or mismatch with the music interaction?

## 5. Sequencer Expansion

- Around June 30 to July 3, 2025 the project expands hard into circular sequencers, knobs, sticky bottoms, melody libraries, and a more complex interface. What problem did the sequencer seem to solve at that moment?
- Did the record-player / circular form feel musically meaningful, or mainly like a promising UX metaphor?
- Was the sequencer supposed to be central, or was it already drifting into "this is cool, but maybe not the app" territory even while you were building it?
- When you look back, what part of that era still deserves to survive in some form?

## 6. The Self-Analysis Phase

- The repo starts generating refactoring docs, architecture analysis, and performance strategy right in the middle of the feature bloom. Did that come from pain, ambition, outside help, or all three?
- Were those docs clarifying the path, or were they signs that the project had become hard to hold in your head?
- How much of that period felt like genuine design thinking, and how much felt like "I need to tame this before I drown in it"?

## 7. The PWA Add-Then-Revert Moment

- On July 31, 2025 you add PWA support and revert it the same day. What exactly happened there?
- Was the problem technical, experiential, conceptual, or just bad timing?
- Did that moment reveal that the app was not ready to be packaged, or that you were not yet sure what version of the app deserved packaging?

## 8. The August Pruning

- August 22, 2025 is the harshest simplification in the history: the sequencer runtime and a large test surface get removed. What truth had become undeniable by then?
- Did removing the sequencer feel relieving, painful, embarrassing, clarifying, or some mix?
- What were you protecting by deleting so much?
- Was there a specific user experience you wanted back once the heavier systems were gone?

## 9. Pattern System V2 And "Clean Slate?"

- Pattern System V2 gets a full architecture document on August 26, 2025, then a big implementation on August 29, then a dramatic "clean slate?" deletion the same day. What happened emotionally and conceptually in that sequence?
- Did you feel the pattern system was the right direction but the wrong implementation, or did the whole concept wobble?
- What does "mostly manual and controlled" mean in the September 6, 2025 commit message? Controlled by you as designer, controlled by the user, or controlled compared with generative behavior?
- Was this the point where the app paused because you were stuck, or because life and adjacent projects pulled you elsewhere?

## 10. Strudel As Turning Point

- When you found Strudel, what did it solve immediately that your own system was struggling to solve?
- Was the appeal mostly notation, playback model, architecture, live-coding adjacency, or the feeling of working with a better musical abstraction?
- Did adopting Strudel make the app feel more itself, or did it temporarily feel like the app was becoming something else?
- Which parts of your custom sequencing work still shaped the Strudel era, even after replacement?

## 11. What Still Feels Unfinished

- You said the end feels near, but not here yet. What remains fundamentally unresolved versus merely unpolished?
- Which old desire still has not found the right implementation: popup, visuals, haptics, keyboard feel, session flow, something else?
- If the project shipped tomorrow, what absence would bother you most because it would misrepresent the app's soul?

## 12. Questions About Naming And Identity

- When did the project stop being Note Pads and become EmotiTone Solfege in your own mind?
- Did the name change reflect a real conceptual shift, or did the concept shift first and only later demand a new name?
- If you had to name the project differently at three stages of its life, what would those names be?
